# Lab 04: Cybercrime Law and Cooperation

**Track:** Ledger | **Level:** Beginner | **Time:** ~60 min (reading)

> General education, not legal advice. Laws change, so always check the current text and your own jurisdiction.

## What is it?
Cybercrime crosses borders in seconds, but investigations don't. The legal toolkit tries to bridge that gap.

## Key instruments
| Instrument | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Budapest Convention on Cybercrime (Council of Europe, 2001)** | First major treaty. Defines offences (illegal access, data interference, computer-related fraud), procedural powers (preservation, production orders) and international cooperation. Joined by many non-European states. |
| **Second Additional Protocol (2022)** | Faster cross-border access to electronic evidence, including direct requests to service providers. |
| **UN Convention against Cybercrime** | Adopted by the UN General Assembly in December 2024 as a global treaty. Check the UN and UNODC pages for current signature and ratification status. |
| **MLATs (Mutual Legal Assistance Treaties)** | Formal government-to-government channel for evidence requests. Reliable but slow. |
| **Police cooperation** | Interpol and Europol coordinate operations and share intelligence. |
| **Data protection law** | Limits how personal data is collected and shared (e.g. GDPR in the EU; Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law No. 151 of 2020). |
| **AML / CFT standards (FATF)** | Require financial institutions and virtual-asset service providers to know customers, monitor transactions and report suspicious activity. |

## Core concepts
- **Jurisdiction:** Which country's law applies when the attacker, victim and servers are in three places?
- **Electronic evidence:** Must be preserved quickly (preservation requests) and handled lawfully (chain of custody, Lab 03).
- **Dual criminality:** Many cooperation requests require the conduct to be a crime in both countries.
- **Proportionality and safeguards:** Investigative powers need legal basis and oversight.
- **Attribution vs. proof:** Technical attribution isn't automatically legal proof.
